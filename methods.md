# METHODS

This study used data from the Family Lifestyle Outcomes Research (FLOR) ancillary study of the Hispanic Community Health Study/Study of Latinos (HCHS/SOL). SOL FLOR enrolled HCHS/SOL women who had a first live singleton birth between the HCHS/SOL baseline visit (2008–2011) and Visit 2 (2014–2017), along with their children, across four U.S. field centers: the Bronx, New York; Chicago, Illinois; Miami, Florida; and San Diego, California. The analytic sample was restricted to FLOR mother–child dyads with complete child anthropometric measurements, yielding a main sample of n = 227 participants. Supplemental analyses were conducted among the n = 201 dyads with an observed child BMI polygenic risk score (PRS). The sequential inclusion and exclusion criteria are detailed in the Supplemental Appendix.

## Statistical Analysis

Maternal preconception and child characteristics were described for FLOR dyads with complete child anthropometry and, for comparison, for non-FLOR participants or those with incomplete anthropometry. Continuous variables are presented as means (standard deviations) and categorical variables as frequencies (percentages). Chi-square tests and one-way ANOVA were used to compare distributions between groups.

The primary outcome was the child's BMI z-score, a measure of BMI standardized for age and sex. Secondary outcomes included weight-for-age z-score (WAZ) and two categorizations of adiposity status: a binary indicator of overweight or obesity (BMI-for-age ≥ 85th percentile) and a three-level BMI category (normal weight, overweight, and obese). As a supplemental analysis, associations were also examined using child birth weight-for-gestational-age z-score.

Maternal preconception socio-behavioral predictors were organized into three domains: (1) sociodemographic and acculturation characteristics, including Hispanic/Latino background, maternal age, parity, education, employment, marital status, health insurance, years in the United States, and field center; (2) health behaviors, including cigarette use, alcohol use, diet quality (assessed with the Healthy Eating Index 2010, categorized into sample-derived tertiles), physical activity (meeting the 2008 Physical Activity Guidelines), and sleep duration; and (3) mental health, including depressive symptoms (CESD-10) and anxiety symptoms (STAI-10). The child's BMI PRS was included as an additional predictor to account for genetic predisposition to adiposity. Time elapsed between the baseline HCHS/SOL visit and the FLOR visit was included as a covariate in all models to account for follow-up timing.

Missing values in covariates and outcomes were handled through multiple imputation using the fully conditional specification approach, generating 10 imputed datasets. The imputation model included all outcomes, all study covariates, and field center. Continuous variables were imputed using linear regression or predictive mean matching; binary variables using logistic regression; and nominal or ordinal variables using polytomous logistic regression. Augmented likelihood was used for sparse categorical variables. Raw child anthropometric measurements and BMI category were not imputed. After imputation, the Healthy Eating Index was categorized into sample-derived tertiles, and sleep duration was dichotomized at 8 hours per night.

Associations between maternal preconception socio-behavioral factors and each child weight outcome were estimated from generalized linear models fit separately within each imputed dataset and pooled using Rubin's rules. Four nested models were built: Model 1 included sociodemographic and acculturation predictors; Model 2 added health behavior predictors; Model 3 further added mental health predictors; and Model 4 added the child's BMI polygenic risk score. Only results from the fully adjusted Model 4 are presented in the main tables.

For continuous outcomes (BMI z-score, WAZ, and birth weight z-score), a normal distribution was assumed and results are reported as regression coefficients (β) and standard errors (SE). The proportion of variance explained by each predictor in the fully adjusted model was quantified using partial R². For the binary outcome (overweight or obese), a binomial model was used and results are presented as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). For the three-level BMI category outcome, a multinomial logistic model was fit with normal weight as the reference category; odds ratios for overweight vs. normal, obese vs. normal, and obese vs. overweight are reported. Supplemental analyses repeated all main models in the PRS-complete subset (n = 201) and examined associations with birth weight-for-gestational-age z-score using the same four-model structure.

Statistical significance was considered at the 0.05 level. All analyses were conducted in SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc).
