METHODS
This study used data from the Hispanic Community Health Study/Study of Latinos (HCHS/SOL), a population-based cohort study that seeks to identify risk factors and disease prevalences rates in Hispanic populations located in four large communities in the United States: the Bronx, San Diego, Chicago and Miami. The HCHS/SOL cohort included 16,415 participants at baseline. However, the current study is based on participants who attended the Visit 3 clinical examination, met the HCHS/SOL diabetes criteria, did not receive dialysis at Visits 2 or 3, and had an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) greater than 20 mL/min/1.73 m² at both visits. Participants who missed a medication scan were excluded from the analysis.  After exclusion, the analytic sample comprised n=2,781 participants. For a detailed examination of the number of participants included and excluded, please see Appendix XXXX. Data on serum creatinine, cystatin C and urine albumin in V3 participants comes from the HCHS/SOL ancillary studies PASOS, SGM/SOL and AS#2021.09 multi-omics biomarkers of chronic kidney diseases. Thus, the analyses involving these biomarkers reflect the subsets available for analysis. Missing values were handled through multiple imputation.
Statistical Analysis 
Univariate analysis for all demographic and health-related variables was employed to characterize the study sample. Design-based weighted percentages, means, and 95% confidence intervals were therefore obtained. The cell count (N) presented corresponds to the unweighted number of participants in the studied cohort. This and subsequent analyses take the HCHS/SOL complex survey design into account, including stratification, clustering, and sampling weights. 
Age-adjusted prevalences of RAASi and GLP1+SGLT2 RA across the characteristics under study were estimated from survey-weighted logistic regression models that adjusted for age groups. The prevalence estimates were therefore derived as predicted least squares means (marginal means) from the fitted models, averaged over the observed distribution of covariates. Two-sided p-values were also calculated for both the overall test of equal prevalences across all levels of the factors and pairwise comparisons between each level and its reference category within a factor. Overall factor effects were assessed using Wald tests.

We estimated associations between participant characteristics and RAASi and GLP-1+SGLT2 RA use from survey-weighted logistic regression models, which were fit separately within each imputed dataset. Two modeling strategies were followed. First, the reduced model included the age group, sex, Hispanic/Latino background, field center, educational level, and health insurance status. Second, the reduced model was adjusted by adding one additional covariate at a time: language preference, years in the United States, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, BMI, waist-to-hip ratio, change in BMI between visits, eGFR <60, HbA1c, and albuminuria. For each factor, an overall p-value was calculated from a multivariate Wald test of the null hypothesis that all odds ratios (ORs) for that factor equal 1, and pooled across imputations. 
As previously mentioned, to address missing key biomarkers and take advantage of participants with complete reported outcomes among those who met the inclusion criteria, we performed multiple imputation using the fully conditional specification approach35 generating ten imputed datasets. The imputation model included both medication outcomes (RAASi and SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA medication), and all the study covariates. Ignoring the sampling design during the imputation might lead biased results36, so the design variables such strata, cluster sizes and sampling weight were also included in the imputation model. A linear, logistic, and multinomial regression model was assumed for continuous, binary, and categorical variables with more than two levels, respectively. Continuous variables with skewed distribution were transformed using Box-Cox transformation before imputation and tabulated estimates were pooled across imputations using Rubin’s rules37. All analyses were conducted in SAS 9.4 software. Statistical significance was considered at 0.05 level.
